# Leo Rozanov

PhD student in Computer Science at University College London (September 2026–present). I completed an MSc in Physics with Extended Research at Imperial College London, graduating with Distinction, and a BA in Physics at the University of Chicago.

I work on machine learning for scientific data. At Imperial, I developed contrastive representation learning and event-level zero-shot learning for CMS Level-1 trigger data, and explored continuous-variable photonic quantum machine learning for jet classification and anomaly detection. Earlier work at Chicago focused on tracker studies and long-lived-particle searches for a 10 TeV muon collider.

## Research

- **CMS Level-1 contrastive learning:** Built a three-stage pipeline combining triplet-loss pretraining, paired-view InfoNCE, and supervised L1 fine-tuning. On a five-class, 10-million-event subset, L1 validation accuracy rose from 62.4% to 65.0%.
- **Event-level zero-shot learning:** At a threshold rejecting 99% of minimum-bias events, median signal retention was 87.2% for BSM, 94.2% for unseen SM, and 85.2% for seen SM samples.
- **FPGA synthesis:** An HGQ Linformer fit within one SLR of a VU13P FPGA; Alkaid synthesis reported 115 ns latency.
- **Quantum machine learning:** Implemented trainable Strawberry Fields photonic circuits for top-quark versus QCD jet classification. One documented run reached 0.799 test ROC AUC on 1,000 held-out jets. I also prototyped an autoencoder for anomaly scoring, with 0.797 test AUC on an initial 500-jet sample.
